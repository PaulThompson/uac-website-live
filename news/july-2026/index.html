Back-country touring, with Gabi Burge

Some of our younger members undertook a circuit late last year and share here some of their excellent photos to encourage the downhillers in the club to venture out just a little wider from the resort areas.

The equipment was Alpine Touring Skis with pin bindings – which are a lot of fun as they are similar to downhill skis, and give a great ride down the hill, while also being very reasonable for skinning up. The route was a loop tour out from Guthega, camping two nights then skiing out from Guthega, across Illawong bridge and up to a camp spot at Twynam Creek (beautiful sunset overlooking Guthega).

The next day was skinned up to the Mount Twynam Trig (Gabi sitting on the trig) and then around the back of Mt Twynam. Skis were then converted into downhill mode to allow a great number of laps in a beautiful sunny bowl, before skiing back to camp across Blue Lake!

The last day, headed north from the campsite up the range with some more great photo opportunities then descending into Guthega pondage for some welcomed hot chocolate.

I hope this inspires some downhill skiers to try out a new type of skiing that takes you to some awesome snow and beautiful views!

Getting fit in June, with Anabel & Arthur Copeman

What can you do in June to get fit ready for our ski season? Here is one intrepid couple’s experience.

As June has not enough snow in Perisher to ski and there is no snow on the other side of the equator, the Copemans (Anabel & Arthur) went cycling in Portugal. The undertaking, an eight-day ride self-guided tour from the Northern city of Porto to Lisbon in the South, a distance of approximately 350km.

Arriving by air into Porto, the adventure got off to a standard Mediterranean start with the hotel forgetting to do the airport pickup. Unperturbed, and after finally settling in to the excellent hotel, the day was spent exploring Porto taking in samples of the local port tastings before embarking on the ride south the next day.

The route took us along the stunning Portuguese coastline on perfectly suited gravel bikes supplied by the tour company – Bikeiberia. The first day was all flat, the only hiccup being that we arrived to catch a ferry across a river at lunchtime just in time for the ferry two-hour lunch!

Each day was filled with varied adventures and unique scenery. Nights on the way were spent in Aveiro (a city of canals and tiled architecture), Figueroa da Foz, Nazare (an infamous surfing mecca, but alias none this trip), Obidos (an ancient walled city), Ericeira and Sintra (a kind of Disneyland of ancient castles on the top of a hill).

Challenges experienced on the way included an electronic navigation system which kept freezing. Thankfully, we had been provided with a good paper map as backup and we are old enough to know how to read one, though still managing to get lost on a number of occasions. A cycle path, which was recently closed and requiring some imaginative re-routing. At times very isolated riding on rough terrain and a day where the weather turned foul necessitating a lift in a large black Mercedes taxi for the final 20km.

We have the most wonderful memories of the entire trip, plus a sense of achievement in safely arriving in Lisbon. We would thoroughly recommend this trip but probably would suggest the e-bike option, as there were numerous hills in the second half of the ride.

Port tasting to relaxing on the coastal route.

Rusutsu – Japan’s not-so-secret powder paradise, roaming with Gary & Lesley Neilson

A visit to Japan’s northern island of Hokkaido in early February 2026 once again proved why Rusutsu has earned its reputation as one of the world’s premier powder destinations. True to form, the resort did not disappoint.

Rusutsu is renowned for its deep, dry powder snow, world-class tree skiing and extensive off-piste terrain. By early February, the snow on the summit had gathered an impressive 300 cm snow base, with 130 cm at village level. Mid-February reports had recorded a season-to-date accumulation of snowfall exceeding 7.5 to 8 metres.

Each morning began with a ritual that quickly became part of the adventure. Overnight snowfalls were so generous that local farmers would arrive to dig us out of our accommodation before we could even think about skiing. Once the roads were cleared, it was a short drive to the East Mountain car park.

The slopes had been perfectly groomed overnight, but it was the untouched powder lying beside the pistes that beckoned. We hurried to the early-opening chairlifts, determined to claim those magical first tracks on East Mountain before anyone else. There is something unforgettable about gliding through untracked powder, each turn sending clouds of light, dry snow billowing into the air, leaving nothing but graceful arcs behind.

Most mornings started with beautiful blue bird skies, providing the perfect backdrop for some of the finest skiing imaginable. Mt Isola quickly became our favourite playground. Wherever the gondolas and quad chairs deposited us, another hidden stash of silky powder seemed to be waiting. Every run tempted us to venture just a little farther, weaving effortlessly through the mountain’s famous glades.

The tree skiing was simply exhilarating. Rusutsu’s beautifully spaced birch and fir forests offered endless opportunities to float through deep, untouched powder. Even the occasional tumble became part of the fun. Landing in waist-deep snow was surprisingly soft, and lying there for a moment brought an unexpected reward – the gentle rustle of snow-covered branches, the occasional birdsong, and an almost complete silence that made the mountain feel wonderfully peaceful. Of course, getting back onto your skis was another matter entirely! Wrestling yourself upright in deep powder became a daily challenge, usually accompanied by plenty of laughter before setting off again for another unforgettable run.

At the end of each afternoon, we would often pause to admire the majestic Mt Yotei rising above the surrounding landscape. Standing there, surrounded by fresh snow and spectacular scenery, it was impossible not to feel incredibly fortunate. Rusutsu had once again delivered everything a skier dreams of – bottomless powder, endless smiles, and memories that will last long after the snow has melted.

Rusutsu – the good, the great and the things to know

After several visits to Rusutsu, here are a few observations for anyone thinking about adding it to their ski bucket list.

Powder… and lots of it! The powder is every bit as good as people say. It’s incredibly light and dry – so dry, in fact, that trying to make a snowball is almost impossible. Forget snowball fights; the snow simply won’t cooperate!

Comfortable chairlifts. The high-speed quad chairs are equipped with footrests and bubble covers, making even the coldest, windiest rides surprisingly comfortable. On storm days, you’ll really appreciate the extra protection.

Flexible lift tickets. One of Rusutsu’s best ideas is the option to buy lift tickets by the hour. If the weather turns nasty, you have not wasted money on a full day or weekly pass. (Epic Pass holders can also ski at Rusutsu.)

We purchased a 25-hour pass, which can later be topped up in 5-hour blocks if needed. The timing system is quite clever. Your remaining hours only decrease after each full hour of skiing, rather than continuously counting down. For example, after your first lift of the day the display will show 24 hours remaining and will continue to show 24 hours until more than one hour has elapsed from that first scan. It’s worth keeping an eye on the clock – you can often squeeze in one more run before stopping for lunch without using another hour!

Great local dining. Unlike some larger ski resorts, Rusutsu still has several small, family-run Japanese restaurants. They’re well worth visiting, although booking ahead is highly recommended as they’re very popular.

A few things to be aware of

Accommodation is not getting any cheaper. The secret is definitely out. As Rusutsu’s reputation continues to grow internationally, accommodation prices have climbed and are likely to keep rising. Booking well in advance is becoming increasingly important.

Tree skiing and insurance. Rusutsu is famous for its outstanding tree skiing, and it’s one of the highlights of the resort. Before heading off piste, however, make sure your travel insurance covers skiing outside marked runs.

Also be aware that if you require assistance while skiing off-piste, ski patrol may require an upfront payment (reported to be around US$500) before commencing a search and rescue operation. It’s another good reason to ski with a partner, carry a phone, know where you’re going, and understand the resort’s off-piste policies before heading into the trees.
